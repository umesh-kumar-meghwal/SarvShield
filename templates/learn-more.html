DIGITAL SAFETY GUIDE

Learn how to
spot deception.

Scams are engineered to trigger emotional reactions before you have time to think. Learn the warning signs, recognize social engineering techniques, and protect yourself against fraud.

COMMON THREAT PATTERNS

Four Common Scam Vectors

01

Phishing

Phishing messages pretend to originate from banks, trusted delivery companies, or coworkers. They contain deceptive links engineered to steal login credentials and financial details.

Urgent requests Fake domains Credential theft
02

Fake Authority

Scammers impersonate police officers, bank fraud executives, tax officials, or customer care agents using spoofed caller IDs to create acute fear and legal panic.

Impersonation Legal threats Immediate pressure
03

Too Good To Be True

Guaranteed high-return investments, fake lottery prizes, unrequested cashback, and unbelievable shopping discounts are classic traps designed to elicit advance fee deposits.

Unexpected rewards Advance fees Fake offers
04

Social Engineering

Rather than technical hacking, fraudsters manipulate psychology: cultivating false intimacy, urgency, curiosity, or sympathy to convince targets into bypassing security safeguards.

Psychological fear Artificial urgency Trust manipulation
DEFENSIVE PLAYBOOK

The Four Golden Rules of Digital Safety

01

Pause.

Never react immediately to alarming or urgent messages. Take a breath and evaluate.

02

Verify.

Independently check the sender's identity through official phone numbers or websites.

03

Protect.

Never share One-Time Passwords (OTPs), card PINs, or confidential login details with anyone.

04

Report.

Submit suspicious activity on ScamShield to protect the global community from repeated attacks.
